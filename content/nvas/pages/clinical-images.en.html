A validated visual repository

Every clinical image published in NVAS joins an open-access collection of validated veterinary images: confirmed diagnosis, peer review, standardized legend, DOI and CC BY 4.0 licence. Images are classified by species, body system or organ, and modality, building a reference for teaching and diagnosis in Neotropical veterinary medicine. Publishing here makes your case a citable reference for colleagues and students.

The format

  • up to 500words per language (text + legends)
  • 1 or 2figures, at most 4 panels (A–D)
  • up to 10words in the title
  • up to 60words in the abstract, per language
  • up to 4authors, including the graduate supervisor
  • up to 5references
  • 3 to 5keywords: species, body system or organ, and modality

Text structure: signalment and history → what the image shows → diagnosis and how it was confirmed → outcome → teaching point.

Which images qualify

  • Original, from a case you handled or studied, never published in a journal or book.
  • Diagnosis visible in the image and confirmed (histopathology, cytology, culture, PCR, advanced imaging, surgery, necropsy or follow-up). Clinical suspicion alone is not enough.
  • Teaches something: a well-documented classic presentation, a rare finding, a diagnostic pitfall, or relevance to One Health, animal production or welfare.
  • Technically good: sharp focus, good light, clean background. For radiology, the exam exported from DICOM, with view or plane and orientation in the legend.
  • Anonymized and with no unnecessary suffering. A consent form from the legal guardian is needed only if the image could identify the animal or its guardian (a teaching hospital admission form also counts).

The photo: do and don't

Do

  • Arrows, arrowheads, asterisks and outlines, few and explained in the legend
  • Panels A, B, C, D combining photo, diagnostic imaging and histopathology
  • Crop, rotate and adjust brightness, contrast and colour of the whole image
  • Scale bar on micrographs; a ruler on gross specimens
  • Mask names, ear tags and logos, stating it in the legend

Don't

  • Screenshots or photos forwarded through WhatsApp (they arrive compressed)
  • Erase, clone, move or add any element
  • Filters, local retouching or artificial intelligence "enhancement"
  • Patient or guardian names, record numbers, dates or identifiable people
  • More than 4 panels

Upload two versions of the figure: labelled and unlabelled. Original file (maximum-quality JPEG, TIFF or PNG; exams exported from DICOM), at least 300 dpi, or 2,000 pixels wide for the assembled figure. Keep the originals: the editors may request them.

How it works

  1. Choose the caseWith a confirmed diagnosis. Anonymized slides, specimens and exams need no consent form. Invite your supervisor as co-author.
  2. Prepare the imageOriginal file, up to 4 panels, one labelled and one unlabelled version.
  3. Write in the templateIn English or Portuguese, up to 500 words. Any words on the image in English.
  4. SubmitChoose the Clinical Images section and upload the files.
  5. Editorial screeningUsually within 7 days: inclusion criteria, quality and anonymization.
  6. Peer reviewTwo reviewers who do not know the authors, using the clinical image form.
  7. Second languageOnce the image is accepted, the journal prepares the text in the other language and you approve it.
  8. PublicationArticle online with a DOI, in English and Portuguese, open access.

How reviewers assess it

Every image is assessed with the same form and objective criteria:

  • Teaching valueDoes the image teach something new or exemplary?
  • Technical qualityFocus, exposure, framing and resolution.
  • DiagnosisRecognizable in the image and confirmed by an appropriate method?
  • Labels and panelsNecessary, clear and explained in the legend.
  • IntegrityLabelled and unlabelled versions compared; no undue editing.
  • Reference valueIs the image a reliable reference for the collection?
  • TextAccurate, concise and with a clear teaching point.
