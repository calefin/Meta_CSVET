3. Reporting guideline checklist: which one to upload

Required for original articles, short communications, case reports and series, reviews, methods and data articles (not needed for clinical images, perspectives and letters to the editor). Download the checklist for your study design, fill in the manuscript page or line where each item is, and upload it with the type “Reporting guideline checklist”. PDF or Word is fine.

  • CARECase reports and case series. Download the CARE checklist. Pathology case (biopsy or necropsy): mark “not applicable” on the clinical items the laboratory did not receivecase report
  • ARRIVE 2.0Animal experiments (treated and control groups, laboratory or field). Download the ARRIVE checklistoriginal article, short communication
  • STROBE-VetObservational studies: cohort, case-control, cross-sectional, surveys. Download the STROBE-Vet checklistoriginal article
  • CONSORT / REFLECTRandomized clinical trials (CONSORT); in livestock and food safety (REFLECT).original article
  • STARDDiagnostic test accuracy (sensitivity, specificity, comparison with a reference standard). Download the STARD checklistoriginal article, method
  • PRISMA 2020 / PRISMA-ScRSystematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA 2020); scoping review (PRISMA-ScR).review
  • SANRANarrative review and mini-review: self-assessment with the SANRA scale (six items, in the original paper). See the SANRA scalenarrative review
  • Another design?Method, data article or another study type: find the guideline at the EQUATOR Network or use the one for the study that generated the data. If in doubt, write to the journal.method, data article

5. From case to submission: the path for first-time authors

  1. Talk to your supervisorChoose the case or study and the article type together. The supervisor (or another graduate professional) is a mandatory co-author, responsible for the work.
  2. Gather the materialClinical records, test results, reports and original photos. Check that the diagnosis was confirmed (histopathology, culture, PCR, imaging or necropsy).
  3. Read 3 to 5 similar articlesSearch PubMed, Google Scholar and SciELO for cases or studies like yours. They go into the Discussion and show how it is written.
  4. Write in the templateStart with what you know best (the case or the methods), then the Discussion and the Introduction. Abstract and title last. Use Zotero for references.
  5. Revise with your supervisorCorrect, delete the guidance boxes and check that no names are left in the manuscript and that all text inside the figures is in English.
  6. Fill in the title page and the letterWith all authors, their ORCID (free at orcid.org) and the supervisor's profile link.
  7. Register and submitChoose the section for your article type, upload each file choosing its type (anonymized manuscript, title page, cover letter, checklist) and tick the authors' declaration in the last step. If a file is missing, the system tells you before you finish.

Reminders that avoid your manuscript being returned

  • English or Portuguese: write the whole manuscript in one language. The journal publishes in English.
  • Figures in English: all text inside figures and graphs (axes, labels, internal legends) must be in English at submission.
  • No names in the manuscript: no authors, supervisor, institution, hospital or city. Names go only on the title page.
  • Original photos: no filters, no AI “enhancement” and no names, dates or record numbers on the image.
  • Ethics: routine clinical cases need no ethics committee approval; consent from the legal guardian is needed only if photos or text allow the animal or owner to be identified.
