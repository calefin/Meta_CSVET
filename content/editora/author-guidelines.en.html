Author Guidelines

Editora CSVET publishes open-access scientific and technical books, edited volumes, manuals, proceedings and monographs (undergraduate, specialisation and residency monographs, master's dissertations and doctoral theses) in veterinary medicine, animal science and related fields. These guidelines explain how to prepare and submit a book proposal. Proposals that do not follow them are returned to the authors before review.

2. Who may submit

  • At least one author or volume editor must hold a degree in veterinary medicine, animal science or a related field. Works authored exclusively by students are not accepted.
  • Undergraduate monographs are submitted after graduation, with the Supervisor's Consent. Supervision and examining committee are credited as described in section 4.
  • All authors must have an ORCID iD (free at orcid.org) and must have approved the submission.

3. Turning an academic work into a book

A monograph, dissertation or thesis is not published as it was defended. Before submitting:

  • remove elements specific to the academic work (approval page, long dedications, extensive lists, repetitive methodology chapters, administrative appendices);
  • rewrite the introduction and conclusion for the book's reader (student, practitioner or researcher), not for the examining committee;
  • give the book its own title that describes the content for people searching the subject;
  • update the literature review and references;
  • if parts of the work have been published as articles, state which ones and how the book extends them; do not reproduce text from published articles without the journal's permission (unless the article licence allows it) and without citing them.

Works deposited in an institutional repository may be published as a book: give the repository link in the proposal.

4. Credits: supervision, examining committee and reviewers

Books derived from academic works have already been through a supervisor and an examining committee. The book is a new edition under the responsibility of those who wrote it, and each contribution is credited accordingly. Register each person with the matching role: Author; Supervisor or Co-supervisor (credited on the title page, origin note and book page, not as authors); Examining committee member (origin note and book page, with consent, not authors). A supervisor who also wrote or critically revised the book version is registered as Author. The publisher's reviewers remain anonymous during review and may be named, with their degree, in the book's credits after publication if they agree. Details: Publish your thesis or monograph as a book.

5. Edited volumes: volume editors and chapter authors

The volume editor submits the edited volume proposal and the chapter invitation spreadsheet in the Edited Volumes series, registered as Volume editor. Once approved, the publisher sends the invitations through the platform; authors who accept are added to the book with their chapter already listed, and deliver their chapter and signed Chapter Author Agreement through the book's Discussions. Every chapter is peer reviewed by the publisher. Full guide: Edited volumes.

6. What to submit

  1. Book proposal (up to 3 pages): title and subtitle; intended series; abstract (up to 250 words) and 3 to 6 keywords; readership; rationale and how the book differs from existing titles; table of contents with a short description of each chapter; short biography of each author (degree, institution, ORCID); for edited volumes, the list of chapters and authors.
  2. Full text in .docx without author identification in the text, headers or file properties (review is double-anonymous). Acknowledgements and identifying information are added after acceptance.
  3. Figures as separate high-resolution files (photographs at least 300 dpi at print size; charts and diagrams in vector format or 600 dpi), numbered, with captions and alternative text in the text (see section 8).
  4. Documents: originality and rights statement signed by all authors; approval record and supervisor's consent (monographs, dissertations and theses); permissions for third-party images and materials; ethics approval where applicable.

7. Preparing the text

  • Language: Portuguese, English or Spanish, language-edited before submission. The publisher may require professional editing before production.
  • Formatting: 12 pt font, 1.5 line spacing, chapter and section headings with hierarchical numbering (1, 1.1, 1.1.1). Do not lay out the text: typesetting is done by the publisher.
  • Nomenclature: scientific names in italics; drugs by generic name (brand and manufacturer in parentheses if needed); SI units.
  • Tables editable (not as images), numbered, with title and source.
  • Citations and references: Vancouver or APA style (books in English) or ABNT NBR 10520:2023 and NBR 6023:2018 (books in Portuguese), applied consistently throughout. Include the DOI whenever available. In edited volumes, references go at the end of each chapter.
  • Chapters in edited volumes: each chapter has a title, an abstract of up to 150 words, keywords and authors with affiliation and ORCID (entered in the submission form, not in the reviewed text).

8. E-book: text ready for digital, accessible reading

Every Editora CSVET book is published in several digital versions, all produced from the same Word file:

  • Full PDFFixed layout, identical to the print edition (16×23 or 21×28 cm), with page numbers for citation. Own ISBN.
  • EPUB 3Reflowable text that adapts to phone screens, with adjustable font size, night mode and read-aloud. Opens in e-book readers, including Kindle. Own ISBN.
  • Chapter PDFsIn edited volumes, each chapter can be read and downloaded on its own, with its own DOI and authors.
  • Read onlineThe book opens right in the browser, on a computer or phone, with no download.

Print on demand, when offered, uses the same PDF and receives a separate ISBN.

The EPUB has no fixed pages and is also read by screen readers used by people with visual impairments (Brazil's Inclusion Act, Law 13,146/2015, art. 68, guarantees them access to books). So, in addition to section 7, the text must follow these rules:

  • Use the template styles (Heading 1 for chapters, Heading 2 and 3 for sections, Caption, Long quotation, Reference). The e-book is built from the styles: manual formatting (bold instead of headings, spaces, tabs, page breaks, text boxes, columns) is lost.
  • Alternative text for every figure: one or two sentences saying what the image shows and why it matters (in Word: right-click the image › Edit Alt Text). For charts, describe the main trend; for clinical photographs, the relevant finding. Purely decorative images are marked as decorative.
  • No "see p. 32": in the EPUB, pages change with the screen. Refer to "Figure 3", "Table 2", "section 4.1" or "Chapter 5"; the publisher turns these cross-references into links.
  • Tables readable on a phone: up to 6 columns, with the first row set as a header row (Table Properties › Row › Repeat as header row). Larger tables should be split or moved to an appendix. Never submit a table as an image.
  • Text outside images: labels, arrows and chart legends should be short; explanations go in the caption or the text, where they can be read, enlarged and searched.
  • Colour is never the only cue: in charts and diagrams, also use labels, patterns or symbols, so the figure works for colour-blind readers and in black-and-white print.
  • Equations in Word's equation editor, not as images.
  • Footnotes sparingly: in the EPUB they become end-of-chapter notes with links back and forth.
  • Links and DOIs complete and working (https://doi.org/...). Videos and supplementary material may be given as a link or QR code, which also works in print.
  • Subject index (technical-scientific books and manuals): list the terms; in the e-book each term links to the relevant section.

E-book checklist before you submit

  • Chapters and sections use the template's Heading 1, 2 and 3 styles
  • Every figure has alternative text and a numbered caption
  • No cross-references to page numbers
  • Editable tables, up to 6 columns, with a header row
  • Charts that do not rely on colour alone
  • References with DOI or link whenever available

The publisher takes care of the rest: PDF layout, EPUB assembly and validation (EPUBCheck), accessibility metadata, cover, cataloguing record, one ISBN per format (PDF, EPUB and print), a DOI for the book and for each chapter, separate chapter files in edited volumes and legal deposit at the National Library of Brazil. Before publication, authors receive the PDF and EPUB proofs for checking.

9. Ethics

Practical summary for authors, volume editors and reviewers: Ethics and integrity (COPE).

  • Research involving animals: state the ethics committee (in Brazil, CEUA under Law No. 11,794/2008 and CONCEA rules), protocol number and date. Routine clinical cases do not require ethics approval but do require owner consent. Wildlife: state the collection permit (SISBIO in Brazil) and, where genetic resources are accessed, the SisGen registration (Law No. 13,123/2015).
  • Research involving people (questionnaires, interviews): state the human research ethics committee approval.
  • Images of patients and people must not identify owners, farms or people without written permission.
  • Plagiarism and similarity: books are screened with similarity software. Text reused from the authors' previous publications must be disclosed and cited.
  • Authorship follows ICMJE/COPE criteria. Contributions that do not justify authorship go in the acknowledgements.
  • Artificial intelligence: AI tools cannot be authors. Any use of generative AI in writing must be described in the proposal (tool, purpose and parts of the text); authors are fully responsible for the content, including checking every reference. AI-generated or AI-altered images are not accepted unless they are the subject of the text.

10. Review and timelines

  1. Screening by the editorial office (documents and requirements): up to 7 days.
  2. Internal review by the Editorial Board (scope, originality, quality, readership): up to 15 days.
  3. Double-anonymous peer review by external experts: up to 30 days after reviewers accept.
  4. Decision: publish, minor revisions, major revisions with re-review, or do not publish. Reviews are sent to the authors.
  5. After acceptance: publishing contract, copyediting, standardisation, typesetting, proofs for the authors, ISBN and cataloguing record (issued by CBL) and publication.

11. Rights, licence and costs

  • Authors retain copyright and grant Editora CSVET, through a publishing contract, the right of first publication and distribution in digital and print formats.
  • Books are published open access under the CC BY 4.0 licence, unless another open licence is agreed in the contract.
  • There are no submission or review fees. Optional production services (language editing, translation, printed copies) are quoted in writing before the contract is signed and never influence the editorial decision.
